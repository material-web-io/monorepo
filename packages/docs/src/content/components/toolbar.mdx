Toolbars display frequently used actions relevant to the current page. A toolbar holds controls such as:

- [Icon buttons](/components/icon-button)
- [Buttons](/components/button)
- A [button group](/components/button-group)
- Fields of your own

For the headline and navigation of a page, use an [app bar](/components/app-bar) instead.

```tsx
import { Icon, IconButton, Toolbar } from '@material-web/react'
```

## Basic toolbar

Write the actions of a toolbar as its children. Choose its look with `variant` and `color`, and its direction with `orientation`.

<Example {...basic} isFlush />

The toolbar has no interactions of its own. Handle each action on its element, such as in the `onPress` of an `IconButton`. See [Handling presses](/components/button#handling-presses).

The `Toolbar` comes in two variants. Set `variant` to `docked` (the default) for a toolbar across the bottom of the window, or to `floating` for a pill over the content.

### Docked toolbar

A docked toolbar is best for global actions that remain the same across multiple pages. It is 64dp tall and as wide as its container.

It is not positioned for you. Place it at the bottom of the window yourself: as the last row of a column, as in the first example, or with `className="fixed inset-x-0 bottom-0 z-navigation"`. Do not use a docked toolbar with another element at the bottom of the window, such as a [navigation bar](/components/navigation-bar).

A docked toolbar spaces its elements evenly, with the first and the last 16dp from its ends.

- **Other spacing:** To space the elements another way, such as in a wider window, set `className`. For example, `justify-center gap-x-space-400` centers them 32dp apart.
- **Flex row:** Set `className="flex"` to lay them out as a flex row instead, where each element is as wide as its content. Use it for a `Divider` between groups, or for an element that grows.

<Example {...dockedSpacing} isFlush />

### Floating toolbar

A floating toolbar is best for contextual actions relevant to the body content or the specific page. It is only as big as its elements.

It is not positioned for you either. Float it yourself, such as with `className="fixed bottom-space-200 left-1/2 -translate-x-1/2 z-fab"`, and keep it 16dp or more from the window's edge, or 24dp for a vertical one. See [Stacking order](/theme/layout#stacking-order).

<Example {...floating} isFlush />

## Elements

A toolbar shows its children in the order written. They can be [icon buttons](/components/icon-button), `Button`s, fields of your own, or a [button group](/components/button-group). Each element takes at least 48dp of the row, and a wider one takes its own width.

<Example {...buttonGroup} />

> **Note:** Avoid square icon buttons in a floating toolbar. Their shape conflicts with the fully rounded shape of its container.

## Disabled

Set `isDisabled` on an element of the toolbar to disable it. The arrow keys skip a disabled element.

## Color

Set `color` to choose between two color schemes:

- `standard` (the default) is a low-emphasis scheme, which keeps the attention on the body content.
- `vibrant` is a high-emphasis scheme that draws attention to the controls, and shows the toolbar in the primary container color. It can also indicate a temporary change in the page, such as entering edit mode.

<Example {...colors} />

The toolbar colors every toggle in it, whatever the toggle's own `variant`: an `IconButton` or a `Button` with `isSelectable`, and the buttons of a `ButtonGroup`. A toggle is flat until it is selected. A selected toggle is shown in a container and keeps its round shape.

## Emphasis

A button that is not a toggle keeps the colors of its own `variant`. To emphasize an action, use a `filled` `IconButton` or a `text` `Button`.

<Example {...emphasis} />

## Elevation

A floating toolbar has a shadow. Set `className="shadow-none"` to remove it, such as over content that is visually distinct.

<Example {...noElevation} />

## Orientation

Set `orientation="vertical"` on a floating toolbar to make it a column, 64dp wide. The up and down arrow keys then move between its elements.

<Example {...vertical} />

- **Width:** To keep a vertical toolbar compact, do not use wide icon buttons in it.
- **Window size:** A vertical toolbar is not recommended for a compact window.

## Toolbar with FAB

To present one high-priority action alongside the actions of a floating toolbar, put a [`Fab`](/components/fab) beside the toolbar, not in it, 8dp away (`gap-space-100`). Choose the FAB's `color`, such as `secondary-container` or `tertiary-container`.

## Toolbar with menu

Elements that do not fit are not moved into an overflow menu. If more actions are needed, add the [menu](/components/menu) yourself: wrap the last `IconButton` and a `MenuPopover` in a `MenuTrigger`.

## Customization

Pass classes in `className` to restyle one toolbar. A class of yours replaces the toolbar's own class for the same property. See [Styling](/get-started/styling) for the classes and the theme.

- **Functions:** The `className`, `style` and `children` of a toolbar can be functions of its `orientation`. See [Children as a function](/get-started/composition#children-as-a-function).
- **Every toolbar:** To restyle all the toolbars at once, change the [theme](/get-started/styling#theme).

## Accessibility

### Use cases

Material [lists](https://m3.material.io/components/toolbars/accessibility#2a8a51ec-8795-46ed-8dd9-87083af17059) what people should be able to do with assistive technology:

- Navigate and activate any actions in the toolbar
- Select a destination from a menu
- Activate a back button
- Maintain access to toolbar controls when the content is scrolled or collapsed

### Interaction and style

- **States:** The toolbar has no states of its own, as Material [notes](https://m3.material.io/components/toolbars/accessibility#b03ebe57-4cae-4b0e-9ea0-93be3a0fbe46): each element shows its own state layer, ripple and [focus ring](/theme/accessibility#focus-ring). An arrow key that moves the focus shows the ring on the element it reaches.
- **Touch target:** Each element is centered in a cell at least 48dp wide and tall, so that the 48dp [touch targets](/theme/accessibility#touch-targets) of its elements do not overlap.
- **Color contrast:** In both `color` schemes, the elements keep 3:1 contrast with what they are on and their labels 4.5:1, a selected toggle on its container included, in the light and dark schemes at every contrast level.
- **Forced colors:** In [forced colors](/theme/accessibility#forced-colors), a floating toolbar keeps an edge, and a selected toggle in it is filled with the system's highlight color.
- **Right to left:** The elements are laid out from the right in a right-to-left direction.

### Focus

- Tab moves the focus into the toolbar once: to its first element, as Material [advises](https://m3.material.io/components/toolbars/accessibility#7d1f56cb-c43c-4d1f-9bfe-696bc77321ac), or to the element focused last. The next Tab leaves the toolbar, and Shift+Tab comes back to the element it left.
- The focus moves through the elements in the order written, which is the order shown.

### Keyboard navigation

| Keys                | Actions                                                              |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tab                 | Move the focus into the toolbar, or out of it                        |
| Left or Right arrow | Move the focus to the previous or next element                       |
| Up or Down arrow    | Move the focus to the previous or next element of a vertical toolbar |
| Space or Enter      | Activate the focused element                                         |

- The arrow keys move between its elements, a text field included. They skip a disabled element, and stop at the first and the last. In a vertical toolbar, the left and right arrow keys do not move the focus.
- The left and right arrow keys are reversed in a right-to-left locale, which is set with an `I18nProvider`, not with `dir`.
- The same keys move through the buttons of a `ButtonGroup` in the toolbar, on to the elements after it and back.

### Labeling elements

- The toolbar has the toolbar role, as Material [advises](https://m3.material.io/components/toolbars/accessibility#3b6eda3b-99a9-463d-96af-0919193b1c19) on the web, and reports its orientation with `aria-orientation`. Name the toolbar with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.
- Label each element as its own component asks, as Material also advises. An `IconButton` needs an `aria-label`: see [Icon button](/components/icon-button#labeling-elements).
- A `ButtonGroup` in the toolbar is a group, or a radio group with single selection. Name it with an `aria-label`.
