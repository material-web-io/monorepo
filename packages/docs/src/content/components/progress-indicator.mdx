Progress indicators show the status of an ongoing process, such as loading an app, submitting a form or saving updates.

- **Determinate** indicators show known progress, and fill from 0% to 100%.
- **Indeterminate** indicators show that a process is happening while the wait time is unknown.

When several items are loading, use a single progress indicator for the group. For a short wait, between 200ms and 5s, prefer a [loading indicator](/components/loading-indicator).

A progress indicator comes as one of two components:

| Part                        | Use                                                                   |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `LinearProgressIndicator`   | A bar, as wide as its container. Place it on the edge of a container. |
| `CircularProgressIndicator` | A ring, in an inline box of its size. Center it in an element.        |

```tsx
import { CircularProgressIndicator, LinearProgressIndicator } from '@material-web/react'
```

## Basic progress indicator

Use a `LinearProgressIndicator` or a `CircularProgressIndicator` to show the progress of a process, and name it with an `aria-label`.

An indicator is determinate unless `isIndeterminate` is set, and its `value` is 0 by default.

## Label

Children are rendered inside the indicator. To show the value in the ring of a circular indicator, pass a function as children and center the text it returns over the indicator, as with `absolute inset-0` in this example. The function receives `percentage`, `valueText` and `isIndeterminate`. See [Children as a function](/get-started/composition#children-as-a-function).

<Example {...valueText} />

To show a label or the value beside a linear indicator, write them next to it, and name the indicator by its visible label with `aria-labelledby`. A linear indicator is as wide as its container, so in a row, put it in an element that takes the room that is left.

<Example {...linearWithLabel} />

### Value text

`valueText` is the text that the indicator reports for its value. An indicator reports its value to assistive technology as a percentage, such as `40%`. Where the progress is not a percentage, set `valueLabel` to the text to report, or set `formatOptions` to format the value as another kind of number, as with React Aria's [ProgressBar](https://react-aria.adobe.com/ProgressBar#value). A circular indicator takes both props too.

### Custom scale

A `value` is kept between `minValue` and `maxValue`, which are 0 and 100 by default. Set them to measure the progress on another scale. The indicator fills by the share of the range that the value has reached: step 2 of 5 fills 40% of the ring. A linear indicator takes `minValue` and `maxValue` too.

## Sizes

Set `size` on a circular indicator to its size in dp, from 24 to 240. A size outside the range is brought back to its nearest end. The stroke and the wave scale with the size.

Without `size`, a circular indicator is 48dp at the default thickness, and a thicker stroke adds to it.

<Example {...sizes} />

## Shapes

Set `shape` to `flat` (the default) or `wavy`, which gives the active indicator a wave. The wavy shape can make a longer process feel less static, and suits a more expressive style.

A wavy linear indicator is as tall as its wave. On a wavy circular indicator, the crests of the wave reach past the indicator's size by the amplitude.

<Example {...shapes} />

On a determinate indicator, the wave shows while the progress is above 10% and below 95%. To change the wave, see [Amplitude and wavelength](#amplitude-and-wavelength).

## Track and stop indicator

The track is the part of the ring or the bar that the active indicator has not reached. Set `showTrack={false}` to remove it, as in a button.

<Example {...circularTrack} />

A determinate linear indicator ends in a stop indicator. Set `showStopIndicator={false}` to remove it, only where the track has a contrast of at least 3:1 with the surfaces around it. An indeterminate or a circular indicator has no stop indicator.

## In a button

In a button, remove the track with `showTrack={false}` and give the indicator the label's color with `className="text-current"`. In a very small button, use the flat shape, since the wave is not as visible at that size.

When the button's label already says what is happening, hide the indicator from assistive technology by wrapping it in an element with `aria-hidden`, and still give the indicator an `aria-label`.

> **Note:** An `aria-hidden` set on the indicator itself is not applied.

<Example {...inButton} />

While the button's action is in progress, set its `isPending` instead: a pending button shows a loading indicator of its own, alone, and ignores presses. See [Loading](/components/button#loading).

## Delaying appearance

To keep an indicator from flashing on a process that ends at once, render it only after the process has run for a moment. In this example, the indicator appears after one second: stop the loading before that, and it is never shown.

## Customization

Pass classes in `className` to restyle one indicator. A class of yours replaces the indicator's own class for the same property. See [Styling](/get-started/styling) for the classes and the theme.

- **Width:** A linear indicator is as wide as its container. Pass a width class, such as `w-48`, to set its width.
- **Color:** Pass a text color class for the active indicator, as in [Color](#color).
- **Track color:** The track is not reached by `className`. To change its color, set the `secondary-container` color role on the indicator. See [Theme](/get-started/styling#theme).
- **Functions:** `children`, `className` and `style` can be functions of `percentage`, `valueText` and `isIndeterminate`. See [Children as a function](/get-started/composition#children-as-a-function).
- **Every indicator:** To restyle all the indicators at once, change the [theme](/get-started/styling#theme).

### Thickness

Set `thickness` to the thickness of the stroke in dp. The default is 4.

<Example {...thickness} />

### Amplitude and wavelength

On a wavy indicator, set `amplitude` and `wavelength` in dp. The amplitude is measured from the center of the resting position to the center of the peak, and the wavelength between two adjacent peaks.

| Indicator             | Amplitude | Wavelength |
| --------------------- | --------- | ---------- |
| Linear, determinate   | 3         | 40         |
| Linear, indeterminate | 3         | 20         |
| Circular              | 1.6       | 15         |

These are the defaults. A wavelength shorter than 8dp, or shorter than the thickness, is raised to that length.

<Example {...wave} />

## Limitations

### Hidden indicators

An indicator does not apply an `aria-hidden` of its own. To hide it from assistive technology, set `aria-hidden` on an element around it, as [in a button](#in-a-button).

### Server rendering

A progress indicator is drawn by script. On a server-rendered page, a linear indicator stays empty and a circular one stands still until the page has hydrated. Where an indicator must move before that, use a [loading indicator](/components/loading-indicator), which does not wait for the script.

## Accessibility

### Use cases

Material [lists](https://m3.material.io/components/progress-indicators/accessibility#4f0c3b38-9d61-4c27-99c0-47631a80aedd) what people should be able to do with assistive technology:

- Navigate to the progress indicator
- Understand what progress the indicator is communicating

### Interaction and style

- **Color contrast:** The active indicator and the stop indicator [need](https://m3.material.io/components/progress-indicators/accessibility#a8aa593a-1982-430e-ad34-c8766f05a278) 3:1 contrast with the surface behind them. Keep that contrast when you change their [color](#color). In a button, give the indicator the label's color and remove the track, as [in a button](#in-a-button).
- **Stop indicator:** Remove the stop indicator only where the track has at least 3:1 contrast with the surfaces around it, so that the end of the track stays easy to see. See [Track and stop indicator](#track-and-stop-indicator).
- **Motion:** With [reduced motion](/theme/accessibility#reduced-motion), a determinate indicator jumps to a new value, and an indeterminate one keeps moving.
- **Forced colors:** In [forced colors](/theme/accessibility#forced-colors), the active indicator and the stop indicator are drawn in the system's text color, and the track in its disabled text color.
- **Right to left:** A linear indicator fills from the right in a [right-to-left](#right-to-left) layout. A circular indicator still fills clockwise.

### Labeling elements

- An indicator has the `progressbar` role, and is not a tab stop. It reports its value as text, `40%` unless `formatOptions` or `valueLabel` change it. An indeterminate indicator reports no value.
- Name the indicator with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`, or with a `Label` from `react-aria-components` among its children. As Material [advises](https://m3.material.io/components/progress-indicators/accessibility#dbe2eaa6-17a8-449b-84d9-45c9fee63058), include the process and the affected content in the label, such as Loading news article or Refreshing page.
- To hide an indicator from assistive technology, set `aria-hidden` on an element around it.
