Cards display content and actions about a single subject. Use a card to contain related elements, such as:

- Images
- Headlines and supporting text
- [Buttons](/components/button)
- [Lists](/components/list) and other components

To show a set of items of the same kind, such as messages or contacts, use a [list](/components/list) instead.

```tsx
import { Card } from '@material-web/react'
```

## Basic card

Write the content of a card as its children, and choose its look with `variant`. A card is a container only, and adds no behavior.

## Content

A card shows its children in a column, in the order written: an image that reaches the card's edges, padded text, a row of buttons. It clips its content to its rounded corners.

- **Padding:** A card has no padding. Add your own to its children, such as `p-space-200` for 16dp from its sides.
- **Size:** A card is as tall as its content. Like any `<div>`, it fills the width of a block container and fits its content in a flex row. Give it a width with a class, such as `w-80`.
- **Direction:** Add `flex-row` to lay the children out in a row.
- **Text:** Text in a card has the `text-body-md` style and the on-surface color. Give a headline its own style, such as `text-title-md`.
- **Children:** The children of a card cannot be a function.

## Primary action

A card itself cannot be pressed. To give a card a primary action, such as a link to a detail page, wrap the content that the action covers in a link or a button of your own inside the card:

- Position it with `relative` and add a [`Ripple`](/components/ripple) as its direct child, for the hover and focus layer and the press ripple.
- Add the [`focus-ring-inset`](/theme/state#focus-ring) class for the focus indicator. It is drawn inside the element, where the card does not clip it.

<Example {...primaryAction} />

Write the card's other buttons outside the primary action, so that each one stays a control and a tab stop of its own.

## Customization

Pass classes in `className` to restyle one card. A class of yours replaces the card's own class for the same property. See [Styling](/get-started/styling) for the classes and the theme.

<Example {...custom} />

- **Color:** Pass a container [color role](/theme/color#color-roles) and its on-color, such as `bg-tertiary-container text-on-tertiary-container`.
- **Shape:** A card has 12dp corners. Pass a class of the [corner scale](/theme/shape#corner-scale) for another radius, such as `rounded-shape-xl`.
- **Elevation:** Pass an [elevation level](/theme/elevation#elevation-levels) for another shadow, such as `shadow-elevation-3`.
- **States:** A card reports no state, so `className` and `style` take a value and cannot be a function.
- **Every card:** To restyle all the cards at once, change the [theme](/get-started/styling#theme).

## Accessibility

### Use cases

Material [lists](https://m3.material.io/components/cards/accessibility#0e4390a4-06ed-4a1d-bb8a-4812559e418c) what people should be able to do with assistive technology:

- Navigate to a card and the elements within a card
- Get appropriate feedback based on input type documented under Interaction & style

### Interaction and style

- **States:** A card has no states of its own: hovering or pressing it changes nothing, as Material [advises](https://m3.material.io/components/cards/accessibility#77f3c9d4-e5e4-4c82-82b4-162a8015d1ab) for a card that holds buttons and links. Each button and link in it shows its own states. For a [primary action](#primary-action), the `Ripple` in your link or button shows a state layer on hover and a ripple on a press, and `focus-ring-inset` shows a [focus ring](/theme/accessibility#focus-ring) for keyboard focus.
- **Color contrast:** On-surface text has 4.5:1 contrast or more with the container of every variant, in light and dark schemes at every [contrast level](/theme/color#contrast). Keep that contrast when you change a card's [colors](#customization).
- **Forced colors:** Every card keeps an edge in [forced colors](/theme/accessibility#forced-colors).
- **Dragging:** A card cannot be dragged by itself. If you make cards draggable or swipeable, such as to reorder or dismiss them, Material [requires](https://m3.material.io/components/cards/accessibility#26b009bd-1ae2-4409-add7-6059827091ee) a single-pointer alternative: offer the same actions in a [menu](/components/menu) or a [bottom sheet](/components/bottom-sheet), opened from a button in the card.

### Focus

- A card cannot be pressed and is not a tab stop.
- Tab moves the focus to each button and link in it, in the order written, so that all of a card's controls come before the next card's, as Material [advises](https://m3.material.io/components/cards/accessibility#e29b1799-d235-4d49-8c35-941624876812).
- A [primary action](#primary-action) is a tab stop of its own. Write it before the card's other buttons, so that the focus reaches it first.

### Keyboard navigation

| Keys           | Actions                                                              |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tab            | Move the focus to the next button or link, through each card in turn |
| Space or Enter | Press the focused button                                             |
| Enter          | Follow the focused link                                              |

### Labeling elements

- A card is a `<div>` with no role. Screen readers read its content in the order written, so write it in reading order, such as a headline, an image, the supporting text and then the buttons.
- Material [advises](https://m3.material.io/components/cards/accessibility#04bf5e71-89ba-4485-8c0c-7f2c09de7bc2) hiding a purely decorative image from screen readers: give it an empty `alt`, as in [Media](#media). For a directly actionable card, it advises the link or the button role by its use: wrap a [primary action](#primary-action) in a link to go to a page, and in a button to take an action.
